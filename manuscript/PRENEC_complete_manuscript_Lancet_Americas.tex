\section*{Summary}

\textbf{Background}\quad
PRENEC used FIT to select participants for colonoscopy, so observed lesion
counts did not directly estimate prevalence. This study estimated patient-level
prevalence and detection expected under universal colonoscopy for low-risk
adenoma, high-risk adenoma, and colorectal cancer.

\textbf{Methods}\quad
Routinely collected records were analysed for public-insurance beneficiaries
aged 50--75 years enrolled in eight regions during 2012--2022. Participants
without a reported family history of colorectal cancer and with at least one
recorded OC-Sensor faecal immunochemical test (FIT) formed the analytical
population. One available FIT was randomly selected per person and classified
as positive at $\geq100$ ng Hb/mL.
Observed detection was corrected for lesion-specific FIT sensitivity and
colonoscopy participation; prevalence was additionally corrected for
colonoscopy sensitivity. Two sets of lesion-specific FIT-sensitivity inputs
were used: PRENEC-derived estimates and literature-based estimates. Uncertainty
was propagated through 10\,000 stratified participant-bootstrap replicates
with separate sensitivity draws.

\textbf{Findings}\quad
Among 31\,104 analytical participants, 22\,837 (73\dec{}4\%) were women. PRENEC-derived
FIT sensitivity was 7\dec{}0\% for low-risk adenoma,
21\dec{}8\% for high-risk adenoma, and 100\dec{}0\% for colorectal cancer.
In assignment A, lesion detection under universal colonoscopy was 22\dec{}3\%
(95\% interval 15\dec{}0--35\dec{}1), 9\dec{}8\%
(7\dec{}4--13\dec{}4), and 0\dec{}299\% (0\dec{}245--0\dec{}466),
respectively. Corresponding prevalence was 27\dec{}9\%
(18\dec{}7--44\dec{}1), 11\dec{}9\% (8\dec{}7--17\dec{}2), and
0\dec{}328\% (0\dec{}268--0\dec{}525). Literature-based prevalence estimates
were 25\dec{}7\%, 11\dec{}3\%, and 0\dec{}443\%; assignment B was similar.

\textbf{Interpretation}\quad
Modelled adenoma prevalence was substantial, whereas colorectal cancer
prevalence was below 1\%. Estimates were similar with PRENEC-derived and
literature-based FIT sensitivity, but uncertainty and transportability
assumptions remain important.

\section*{Research in context}

\textbf{Evidence before this study}\quad
Colorectal lesion prevalence has been estimated from autopsy series and
cross-sectional colonoscopy studies, but estimates vary with participant
selection, lesion definitions, and incomplete detection at colonoscopy.
Published Latin American evidence has mainly described adenoma detection
rather than underlying prevalence. Chilean evidence has been limited to a
small primary-colonoscopy study and partial reports from PRENEC.

\textbf{Added value of this study}\quad
This study used a large public-sector screening cohort from eight Chilean
regions and explicitly separated three quantities: lesion yield among
colonoscopy completers, population detection after correction for FIT
sensitivity and colonoscopy participation, and prevalence after additional
correction for colonoscopy sensitivity. PRENEC-derived and literature-based
FIT-sensitivity inputs were compared, and all principal sources of parameter
uncertainty, including colonoscopy participation, were propagated.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
\textbf{Implications of all the available evidence}\quad
Local model-based estimates can inform the expected yield and colonoscopy
requirements of colorectal cancer screening in Chile. Their use in policy or
cost-effectiveness models should retain uncertainty around FIT and
colonoscopy sensitivity and should not assume national representativeness
without additional standardisation.

\section*{Introduction}

Colorectal cancer incidence and mortality vary markedly across countries,
with Chile historically reporting lower rates than many high-income
settings.\cite{Arnold2017,IARC2026} These differences raise questions about
the applicability of international estimates of adenoma prevalence to the
Chilean population. Cancer rates reflect both the occurrence of precursor
lesions and their progression, and cannot establish adenoma prevalence
directly. Local estimates are therefore needed to inform models of colorectal
cancer natural history and screening policy. These parameters are important
for predicting screening yield, planning colonoscopy provision, and
evaluating the cost-effectiveness of alternative screening strategies.

The prevalence of colorectal precursor lesions and cancer has been
investigated through autopsy studies and cross-sectional studies of
participants undergoing colonoscopy.\cite{Wang2023,Heitman2009} Estimates
vary with age, participant selection, lesion definitions, and ascertainment
methods. Colonoscopy studies also underestimate true prevalence when lesions
remain undetected.\cite{Zhao2019} A systematic review and meta-analysis by
Montalvan-Sanchez and colleagues reported adenoma detection rates in Latin
America broadly comparable to those in high-income countries: 19\dec{}9\%
with primary screening colonoscopy and 39\dec{}0\% after a positive
FIT.\cite{Montalvan2024}

In Chile, no autopsy-based estimates of colorectal lesion prevalence have
been identified, whereas primary screening colonoscopy has been studied only
in a small private-clinic sample in Santiago, limiting inference about the
general adult population.\cite{Silva2011}

PRENEC was a large public-sector colorectal cancer screening pilot developed
through collaboration between Chile and Japan.\cite{Okada2016} It enrolled
asymptomatic beneficiaries of the public health insurance system (FONASA)
aged 50--75 years across eight regions during 2012--2022. Existing reports
described programme implementation and screening performance but did not
estimate underlying lesion prevalence or population detection under
universal colonoscopy. Because the programme used FIT to select most
participants for colonoscopy, its observed lesion counts required explicit
modelling of incomplete ascertainment.

This study aimed to estimate the prevalence of low-risk adenoma, high-risk
adenoma, and colorectal cancer, and the population detection expected under
universal colonoscopy, among PRENEC participants without a family history of
colorectal cancer. A probabilistic framework linked observed detection to
FIT sensitivity, colonoscopy participation, and colonoscopy sensitivity.
Analyses used PRENEC-derived and literature-based FIT sensitivities and
were conducted for both sexes combined and separately for women and men.

\section*{Methods}

\subsection*{Study design and participants}

This retrospective analysis used routinely collected records from PRENEC, a
multicentre colorectal cancer screening programme operating in eight Chilean
regions. PRENEC enrolled asymptomatic FONASA beneficiaries aged 50--75 years
between 2012 and 2022; records of first colonoscopies were available through
March, 2023. The estimands pertained to the eligible PRENEC population and
were not interpreted as nationally representative.

Participants without a reported family history of colorectal cancer formed
the source screening cohort. The analytical target population additionally
required at least one recorded FIT result, because participants without a FIT
could not enter the observed detection pathway. Participants with a family
history were excluded from the target prevalence denominator but provided an
internal cohort for estimating FIT sensitivity because colonoscopy was offered
irrespective of FIT results. No extrapolation from participants with a recorded
FIT to those without one was attempted.

PRENEC supplied two OC-Sensor FIT kits. Under the programme protocol,
colonoscopy was offered if either result was at least 100 ng Hb/mL. In the
present study, this criterion was applied only to descriptive programme-level
analyses; for the modelled analyses, one available FIT result was randomly
selected for each participant to determine FIT positivity.

Two complementary FIT assignments, A and B, were generated. When two results were available, one was allocated
to A at random and the other to B; when only one result was available, both
assignments used it. The assignments contained the same participants and
were not independent samples. Assignment A was prespecified for narrative
reporting and assignment B was retained as a robustness analysis.

A qualifying colonoscopy occurred from the first colonoscopy-check date
(inclusive) to the second colonoscopy-check date (exclusive). When the second
date was missing, no upper boundary was applied. The first qualifying
colonoscopy was used.



\subsection*{Target quantities}
The study aimed to estimate lesion prevalence in the average-risk population and lesion detection under universal colonoscopy. Let $N$ denote age-eligible participants without a family history of colorectal cancer who had at least one available FIT result. The target quantities were defined as follows:

\textit{Lesion prevalence.}\quad Let $N_j$ denote the unobserved number of participants with at least one true lesion of category $j$. Following figure~\ref{fig:pathway}, $L_j$ denoted true lesion presence and prevalence corresponds to
\begin{equation*}
p_j=\frac{N_j}{N}=\Pr(L_j).
\end{equation*}

\textit{Lesion detection under universal colonoscopy.}\quad Let $R_j$ denote the counterfactual expected number of participants in whom a lesion of category $j$ would be detected if all underwent colonoscopy. Its corresponding person-level detection rate is $r_j$:
\begin{equation}
R_j=N\,p_j\,\Se_{\mathrm{COL},j},
\qquad
r_j=\frac{R_j}{N}=p_j\,\Se_{\mathrm{COL},j},
\label{eq:truer}
\end{equation}
where $\Se_{\mathrm{COL},j}$ denotes colonoscopy sensitivity for category $j$.

Lesion prevalence, $p_j$, cannot be determined directly even when all participants undergo colonoscopy, because imperfect colonoscopy sensitivity leaves some lesions undetected. Estimating prevalence therefore requires correction for colonoscopy sensitivity. In PRENEC, estimating prevalence presents an additional challenge: $R_j$ cannot be observed directly because not all average-risk participants underwent colonoscopy. Its estimation requires correction for FIT sensitivity and adherence to colonoscopy after a positive FIT.

\subsection*{Detection pathway}
Figure~\ref{fig:pathway} describes the pathway through which a lesion was detected in PRENEC. Let $D_j$ denote the target count for the FIT-based pathway and $d_j$ its population detection probability:
\begin{equation*}
d_j=\frac{D_j}{N}.
\end{equation*}
Participants contributed to this pathway if they had at least one true lesion of category $j$, their selected FIT was positive, colonoscopy was completed, and the lesion was detected with histological confirmation. All counts were person-level.

The FIT--colonoscopy--histology pathway was treated as a conditional sequential-testing process in which the performance of the complete sequence is determined by stage-specific probabilities among participants reaching each subsequent stage.\cite{Fanshawe2024} Consistent with this framework, the detection pathway and its relation to prevalence are:
\begin{align*}
d_j&=p_j\,\Se_{\mathrm{FIT},j}\,
\bigl(\Adh\mid\Se_{\mathrm{FIT},j}\bigr)\,
\bigl(\Se_{\mathrm{COL},j}\mid\Adh,\Se_{\mathrm{FIT},j}\bigr),
\\
p_j&=\frac{d_j}
{\Se_{\mathrm{FIT},j}\,
\bigl(\Adh\mid\Se_{\mathrm{FIT},j}\bigr)\,
\bigl(\Se_{\mathrm{COL},j}\mid\Adh,\Se_{\mathrm{FIT},j}\bigr)}.
\end{align*}
Here, $\Se_{\mathrm{FIT},j}$ was the probability of a positive selected FIT at the 100 ng Hb/mL threshold among participants with at least one true lesion of category $j$. $\Adh$ was the probability of completing colonoscopy after a positive selected FIT. $\Se_{\mathrm{COL},j}$ was the probability of detecting at least one lesion of category $j$ at colonoscopy among participants with a true lesion of that category. The conditional term $(\Adh\mid\Se_{\mathrm{FIT},j})$ represented adherence after FIT selection; $(\Se_{\mathrm{COL},j}\mid\Adh,\Se_{\mathrm{FIT},j})$ represented colonoscopy sensitivity after selection through FIT positivity and colonoscopy completion.

\subsection*{Identification assumptions}
The principal identification assumption was conditional independence between the successive components of the detection pathway. Within each sex and age stratum, colonoscopy adherence was assumed not to vary with lesion-specific FIT sensitivity, and colonoscopy sensitivity was assumed not to vary with either adherence or FIT sensitivity:
\begin{align*}
\bigl(\Adh\mid\Se_{\mathrm{FIT},j}\bigr)&=\Adh,\\
\bigl(\Se_{\mathrm{COL},j}\mid\Adh,\Se_{\mathrm{FIT},j}\bigr)&=\Se_{\mathrm{COL},j}.
\end{align*}
These assumptions allowed the conditional pathway probabilities in figure~\ref{fig:pathway} to be replaced by the corresponding adherence and colonoscopy-sensitivity inputs, giving
\begin{equation*}
d_j=p_j\,\Se_{\mathrm{FIT},j}\,\Adh\,\Se_{\mathrm{COL},j}.
\end{equation*}


Several additional assumptions were required. Within each sex and age stratum, measured adherence among selected-FIT-positive participants was assumed applicable to those with a true lesion. Colonoscopy sensitivity was assumed applicable both to FIT-selected examinations and to universal colonoscopy. FIT-sensitivity estimates were assumed transportable from the family-history cohort or published studies to the average-risk target population, and colonoscopy-sensitivity estimates were assumed transportable from the source studies to PRENEC; these assumptions required sufficiently comparable screening procedures and lesion definitions. The plausibility of these assumptions and the implications of potential departures from them are considered in the Discussion.


\subsection*{Estimators of prevalence and population detection}
Given the assumptions described above, both target quantities could be recovered from FIT-based screening data such as PRENEC.

\textit{Lesion prevalence.}\quad Prevalence was estimated from the empirical detection proportion. Under the identification assumptions, the empirical analogue of the detection pathway was
\begin{equation}
d_j=\frac{ D_j}{N}
= p_j\,\Se_{\mathrm{FIT},j}\,\Adh\,\Se_{\mathrm{COL},j}.
\label{eq:dhat}
\end{equation}
Solving equation~\eqref{eq:dhat} for prevalence can be written as \footnote{Equation~\eqref{eq:p} can be interpreted as the sequential-testing analogue of the Rogan--Gladen estimator, which recovers true prevalence from an observed positive proportion by correcting for imperfect sensitivity and specificity.\cite{RoganGladen1978} In the present setting, the ``test'' was the complete FIT-positive, colonoscopy-completed, and histologically confirmed pathway. Because this complete pathway was assumed to have effectively perfect specificity, the Rogan--Gladen expression reduces to equation~\eqref{eq:p}. The derivation is provided in the supplementary methods.
}
\begin{equation}
 p_j=\frac{ d_j}{\Se_{\mathrm{FIT},j}\,\Adh\,\Se_{\mathrm{COL},j}}.
\label{eq:p}
\end{equation}




\textit{Lesion detection under universal colonoscopy.}\quad Equation~\eqref{eq:truer} gives $r_j=p_j\Se_{\mathrm{COL},j}$. Replacing $p_j$ in equation~\eqref{eq:truer} with its estimator from equation~\eqref{eq:p} yields the FIT- and colonoscopy-participation-adjusted population detection rate:
\begin{equation*}
r_j= p_j\,\Se_{\mathrm{COL},j}
=\frac{ d_j}{\Se_{\mathrm{FIT},j}\,\Adh}.
\end{equation*}
This rate retained losses from imperfect colonoscopy sensitivity. The corresponding estimated count under universal colonoscopy was $\widehat R_j=N\widehat r_j$.

\textit{Observed colonoscopy detection rate.}\quad Additionally, the observed colonoscopy detection rate among participants completing the examination was estimated as
\begin{equation*}
\mathrm{CDR}_j=\frac{D_j}{C},
\end{equation*}
where $C$ was the number of selected-FIT-positive participants who completed colonoscopy. This descriptive measure summarised findings among colonoscopy completers without sensitivity or adherence correction. The framework was applied separately to each lesion category for both sexes combined, women, and men (figure~\ref{fig:pathway}).



\subsection*{Information sources}
\paragraph{Lesion ascertainment and classification.}
Lesions were characterised using the Paris endoscopic classification and assessed histologically according to the Vienna classification and the standardised PRENEC pathological protocol\cite{Paris2003,Vienna2000,Ito2016,Okada2016}. Analyses were conducted at patient level because PRENEC recorded the characteristics of the highest-risk lesion when multiple lesions were identified. Adenomas were identified histologically and classified by recorded size, histological type, and grade of dysplasia; colorectal cancer was identified from the recorded histopathological cancer diagnosis.

Outcomes comprised any adenoma; small (1--5 mm), medium (6--9 mm), and large ($\geq10$ mm) adenomas; non-advanced/low-risk and advanced/high-risk adenomas; and colorectal cancer. Any adenoma was identified directly from the patient record; category sums were consistency checks. Size and risk classifications referred to the highest-risk recorded lesion. The programme's high-risk category included adenomas larger than 1 cm irrespective of histology, villous adenomas, tubular adenomas with high-grade dysplasia, and intramucosal carcinoma. Low-risk lesions were tubular adenomas smaller than 1 cm with low-grade dysplasia. Adenoma number did not enter risk classification.

\paragraph{FIT sensitivity.}
Although sensitivity is commonly treated as a characteristic of the test and, unlike predictive values, is not determined directly by disease prevalence, it is not necessarily invariant across populations or implementation settings because patient spectrum and test execution can affect diagnostic accuracy.\cite{Cohen2016} For FIT, pre-analytical conditions such as sample transport and ambient temperature may reduce sensitivity through degradation of faecal haemoglobin.\cite{Doubeni2016} PRENEC-derived sensitivities were therefore retained alongside literature-based estimates to represent performance under local programme conditions as well as external evidence.

PRENEC-derived patient-level sensitivities were estimated in participants with a family history who had FIT and colonoscopy results. Because colonoscopy was offered irrespective of FIT positivity, eligibility for verification was not restricted to FIT-positive participants. For each lesion category, sensitivity was the proportion with a positive randomly selected FIT among those classified in that category at colonoscopy, with 95\% Wilson confidence intervals. Applying these sensitivities to the average-risk population assumed transportability between the two groups.

The alternative analysis used literature-based FIT sensitivities. A published meta-analysis supplied estimates for advanced adenoma and colorectal cancer\cite{Lin2021}. In the absence of suitable pooled estimates for any adenoma, low-risk adenoma, and adenoma size categories, de novo meta-analyses used study-level information identified in systematic reviews. Synthesis models and available inputs are summarised in supplementary table S2. This analysis replaced only the corresponding outcome-specific FIT sensitivity; other inputs remained unchanged. Thresholds were retained in their original units without assuming equivalence between ng Hb/mL and $\mu$g Hb/g faeces.



\paragraph{Colonoscopy sensitivity.}
Colonoscopy sensitivities were informed by published diagnostic-accuracy evidence, including tandem-colonoscopy studies\cite{Martin2013,vanRijn2006,Zhao2023}. Where evidence did not correspond directly to PRENEC's patient-level categories, indirect size mappings or explicit proxies were used (supplementary table S1). These inputs were used to estimate prevalence from the adjusted population detection rate, not to correct the observed colonoscopy detection rate.

\paragraph{Colonoscopy adherence.}
Adherence was estimated in the average-risk population as the proportion of selected-FIT-positive participants who completed colonoscopy:
\begin{equation*}
\Adh=\frac{C}{F^+},
\end{equation*}
where $F^+$ denoted participants positive on the selected FIT. Estimates were
calculated separately by FIT assignment, sex, and age group (50--54, 55--59,
60--64, 65--69, 70--74, and 75 years). Wilson intervals were descriptive.


\subsection*{Statistical analysis}

All quantities were estimated for both sexes combined and separately for women
and men. Age-specific observed detection was corrected for colonoscopy
participation and aggregated using the age distribution of the analytical
population. Assignment A was used for primary reporting and assignment B as a
paired robustness analysis.

Sampling uncertainty in the screening programme data was quantified using
10\,000 non-parametric participant-bootstrap replicates stratified by sex and
age group. In each bootstrap replicate, FIT positivity, colonoscopy
participation ($C^{(b)}/F^{+(b)}$), and lesion detection were recalculated from
the resampled participants, and the participation correction was applied using
these replicate-specific counts. Resampling participants in this
way also respected the sequential structure of the observed pathway: lesion
detections occurred among colonoscopy completers, who were themselves a subset
of FIT-positive participants. The algebraic equivalence between the explicit
participation correction and an expression based on FIT positivity and lesion
yield among colonoscopy completers, together with full bootstrap details, is
provided in the Supplementary Methods.

The same participant resamples were used for both FIT assignments, all lesion
categories, both sexes-combined and sex-specific estimates, and both sources
of FIT sensitivity. FIT- and colonoscopy-sensitivity draws were
generated independently of the participant resamples and of one another from
their prespecified uncertainty distributions.

Point estimates were plug-in estimates obtained from the observed counts and
point sensitivity estimates. Ninety-five percent uncertainty intervals were
the 2\dec{}5th and 97\dec{}5th percentiles of the joint uncertainty
distribution; simulated estimates were not truncated at 1. To quantify the
relative contribution of each uncertainty source, separate runs varied the
screening data, FIT sensitivity, or colonoscopy sensitivity. Contributions
were expressed as percentages of the additive component variances on the
logarithmic scale; the derivation is provided in the Supplementary Methods and
results are shown in figure~\ref{fig:variance}.
Seeds were prespecified for FIT
selection, participant resampling, and sensitivity draws. Analyses were
performed in R and structured for reporting with STROBE and RECORD; parameter
provenance followed relevant GATHER principles.\cite{STROBE2007,RECORD2015,GATHER2016}

\section*{Results}

\subsection*{Study population and screening outcomes}

The source cohort comprised 34\,241 PRENEC participants aged 50--75 years
without a reported family history of colorectal cancer; 25\,014 (73\dec{}1\%)
were women and mean age was 60\dec{}8 years (95\% CI 60\dec{}7--60\dec{}8;
table~\ref{tab:screening}). Of these, 31\,104 participants had at least one
recorded FIT and formed the analytical population; 22\,837 (73\dec{}4\%) were
women. Both FIT rounds were recorded for 31\,095 participants. A concentration at or above
100 ng Hb/mL was recorded in 8\dec{}8\% in round 1 and 8\dec{}9\% in round
2; concentrations were higher among men.

At least one programme FIT was positive for 4\,087 participants, of whom
3\,433 (84\dec{}0\%) completed a qualifying colonoscopy. There were 3\,434
qualifying colonoscopies in total, including one in a participant without a
positive result in either recorded round. Adequate bowel preparation was
recorded for 79\dec{}4\%, caecal intubation for 95\dec{}0\%, and a withdrawal
time of at least 8 min for 90\dec{}2\%. In the descriptive programme pathway,
low-risk adenoma was found in 18\dec{}1\%, high-risk adenoma in 22\dec{}1\%,
and colorectal cancer in 2\dec{}4\% of qualifying colonoscopies. Detection
was numerically higher among men.

\subsection*{Sensitivity estimates}

PRENEC-derived FIT sensitivity was 7\dec{}0\% (95\% CI
4\dec{}6--10\dec{}4) for low-risk adenoma and 21\dec{}8\%

\subsection*{Relationship with the Rogan--Gladen correction}

Let $Z_j=1$ denote completion of the positive sequential pathway: the
selected FIT was positive, colonoscopy was completed, and lesion category
$j$ was detected and histologically confirmed. Thus
$d_j=\Pr(Z_j=1)$. Defining

\begin{equation*}
\Se_{\mathrm{seq},j}=\Pr(Z_j=1\mid L_j=1)
\end{equation*}

and

\begin{equation*}
\Sp_{\mathrm{seq},j}=\Pr(Z_j=0\mid L_j=0),
\end{equation*}

the law of total probability gives

\begin{equation}
d_j=p_j\Se_{\mathrm{seq},j}
+(1-p_j)(1-\Sp_{\mathrm{seq},j}).
\label{eq:total}
\end{equation}

The Rogan--Gladen inversion is

\begin{equation}
p_j=
\frac{d_j+\Sp_{\mathrm{seq},j}-1}
{\Se_{\mathrm{seq},j}+\Sp_{\mathrm{seq},j}-1}.
\label{eq:rg}
\end{equation}

Under the conditional assumptions in the main Methods,

\begin{equation*}
\Se_{\mathrm{seq},j}
=\Se_{\mathrm{FIT},j}\,\Adh\,\Se_{\mathrm{COL},j}.
\end{equation*}

Because a positive final outcome required histological confirmation, the
sequence was treated as effectively specific:
$\Sp_{\mathrm{seq},j}\approx1$. Equation~\eqref{eq:rg} then becomes

\begin{equation*}
p_j=
\frac{d_j}
{\Se_{\mathrm{FIT},j}\,\Adh\,\Se_{\mathrm{COL},j}},
\qquad
r_j=
\frac{d_j}{\Se_{\mathrm{FIT},j}\,\Adh}.
\end{equation*}

The PRENEC estimators are therefore the effectively perfect-final-specificity
case of the Rogan--Gladen correction applied to the complete
FIT--colonoscopy--histology pathway. FIT specificity affects the number of
colonoscopies generated but cannot create a histologically confirmed adenoma
or cancer in the numerator.

\subsection*{Relationship between the adherence correction and the joint
bootstrap}

For sex-by-age stratum $h$, let $N_h$ denote the analytical population,
$F_h^+$ the number with a positive selected FIT, $C_h$ the number of
FIT-positive participants completing a qualifying colonoscopy, and $D_{j,h}$
the number in whom lesion category $j$ was detected and confirmed
histologically. Estimated colonoscopy participation was

\begin{equation*}
\widehat{\Adh}_h=\frac{C_h}{F_h^+}.
\end{equation*}

The observed population detection proportion was $D_{j,h}/N_h$. Retaining the
adherence correction explicitly, the corresponding empirical pathway
component was

\begin{equation}
\widehat Q_j
=
\sum_h w_h
\frac{D_{j,h}/N_h}{C_h/F_h^+},
\qquad
w_h=\frac{N_h}{N}.
\label{eq:q-adherence}
\end{equation}

Within each stratum, equation~\eqref{eq:q-adherence} has the exact algebraic
representation

\begin{align}
\frac{D_{j,h}/N_h}{C_h/F_h^+}
&=
\left(\frac{F_h^+}{N_h}\right)
\left(\frac{D_{j,h}}{C_h}\right),
\\
\widehat Q_j
&=
\sum_h w_h
\underbrace{\left(\frac{F_h^+}{N_h}\right)}_{\text{FIT positivity}}
\;\times\;
\underbrace{\left(\frac{D_{j,h}}{C_h}\right)}_{\text{yield among colonoscopy completers}}.
\label{eq:q-yield}
\end{align}

Thus, correcting observed detection by participation and multiplying FIT
positivity by colonoscopy yield are two expressions of the same estimator.
Under the identification assumptions described in the main Methods,

\begin{equation*}
\widehat r_j
=\frac{\widehat Q_j}{\Se_{\mathrm{FIT},j}},
\qquad
\widehat p_j
=\frac{\widehat Q_j}
{\Se_{\mathrm{FIT},j}\Se_{\mathrm{COL},j}},
\end{equation*}

where $\widehat r_j$ is lesion detection under universal colonoscopy and
$\widehat p_j$ is lesion prevalence.

The algebraic identity does not imply that colonoscopy participation is
irrelevant. It relies on the assumption that, within each stratum,
colonoscopy yield among completers is applicable to all participants with a
positive selected FIT. Differential participation according to lesion status
could therefore bias both representations. Moreover, incomplete participation
reduces $C_h$ and consequently increases sampling uncertainty. The identity
does imply that participation should not be sampled independently of observed
detection: $D_{j,h}$ is nested within $C_h$, and $C_h$ is nested within
$F_h^+$. Independently drawing $D_{j,h}/N_h$ and $C_h/F_h^+$ would disregard
their shared participants and could misrepresent uncertainty.

\subsection*{Joint participant-bootstrap implementation}

The analytical population comprised participants aged 50--75 years without a
reported family history of colorectal cancer and with at least one observed
FIT result. Participants were resampled with replacement within the 12
sex-by-age strata defined by sex and age groups 50--54, 55--59, 60--64,
65--69, 70--74, and 75 years. Computationally, this was implemented by drawing
multinomial counts over the distinct joint participant patterns observed
within each stratum, which is equivalent to an ordinary stratified
non-parametric participant bootstrap.

Stratum sizes and, consequently, the analytical-population weights $w_h$ were
held fixed. In every replicate $b$, the nested counts
$F_h^{+(b)}$, $C_h^{(b)}$, and $D_{j,h}^{(b)}$ were reconstructed jointly and

\begin{equation*}
Q_j^{(b)}
=
\sum_h w_h
\frac{D_{j,h}^{(b)}/N_h}
     {C_h^{(b)}/F_h^{+(b)}}
=
\sum_h w_h
\left(\frac{F_h^{+(b)}}{N_h}\right)
\left(\frac{D_{j,h}^{(b)}}{C_h^{(b)}}\right)
\end{equation*}

was calculated. A single bank of 10\,000 participant resamples was reused
across assignments A and B, lesion categories, reported populations, and the
two sources of FIT sensitivity. This preserved the empirical dependence of comparisons
made using the same participants.

Sensitivity uncertainty was introduced independently of the target-population
bootstrap. PRENEC-derived FIT sensitivities followed Jeffreys beta
distributions,

\begin{equation*}
\Se_{\mathrm{FIT},j}^{(b)}
\sim
\BetaDist(\mathrm{TP}_j+0\dec{}5,\mathrm{FN}_j+0\dec{}5).
\end{equation*}

This included $\BetaDist(6\dec{}5,0\dec{}5)$ for colorectal cancer, based on
six FIT-positive cases and no observed false-negative cases.
Literature-based FIT sensitivities followed the beta or logit-normal
distributions specified for their respective evidence sources. Colonoscopy
sensitivities followed the prespecified beta distributions derived from the
external evidence. These sensitivity draws were independent of the
participant bootstrap and of one another.

For the full uncertainty analysis, each replicate was calculated as

\begin{equation*}
r_j^{(b)}
=
\frac{Q_j^{(b)}}{\Se_{\mathrm{FIT},j}^{(b)}},
\qquad
p_j^{(b)}
=
\frac{Q_j^{(b)}}
{\Se_{\mathrm{FIT},j}^{(b)}
 \Se_{\mathrm{COL},j}^{(b)}}.
\end{equation*}

Separate runs varied only the screening data, only FIT sensitivity, only
colonoscopy sensitivity, or all applicable components. Colonoscopy sensitivity
was structurally absent from lesion detection under universal colonoscopy and
was varied only for prevalence. Point estimates used the observed counts and
point sensitivity estimates; bootstrap draws were used to construct
uncertainty intervals.

\subsection*{Variance decomposition}

Variance was decomposed on the logarithmic scale because the estimators combine
their inputs multiplicatively. For each lesion, source of FIT sensitivity,
population, and assignment, the component variances were estimated from the
corresponding draws as

\begin{align*}
V_{Q,j}
&=\operatorname{Var}_b\{\log(Q_j^{(b)})\},
\\
V_{\mathrm{FIT},j}
&=\operatorname{Var}_b
\{\log(\Se_{\mathrm{FIT},j}^{(b)})\},
\\
V_{\mathrm{COL},j}
&=\operatorname{Var}_b
\{\log(\Se_{\mathrm{COL},j}^{(b)})\}.
\end{align*}

Because the participant bootstrap, FIT-sensitivity draws, and
colonoscopy-sensitivity draws were generated as independent input blocks,

\begin{align}
\operatorname{Var}\{\log(\widehat r_j)\}
&=V_{Q,j}+V_{\mathrm{FIT},j},
\label{eq:var-detection}
\\
\operatorname{Var}\{\log(\widehat p_j)\}
&=V_{Q,j}+V_{\mathrm{FIT},j}+V_{\mathrm{COL},j}.
\label{eq:var-prevalence}
\end{align}

The minus signs arising from division by sensitivity do not alter the
individual variance terms, and covariance terms are zero under the
independence assumption. The screening-data component $V_{Q,j}$ jointly
contains sampling uncertainty in FIT positivity, colonoscopy participation,
and lesion yield; adherence was therefore not assigned an additional,
independent variance component.

For an estimand with total attributable log variance $V_{\mathrm{tot},j}$, the
percentage contribution from source $k$ was

\begin{equation*}
S_{k,j}
=100\,
\frac{V_{k,j}}{V_{\mathrm{tot},j}}.
\end{equation*}

For lesion detection under universal colonoscopy,
$V_{\mathrm{tot},j}=V_{Q,j}+V_{\mathrm{FIT},j}$; for prevalence,
$V_{\mathrm{tot},j}=V_{Q,j}+V_{\mathrm{FIT},j}+V_{\mathrm{COL},j}$. The
shares therefore sum to 100\% by construction. The log variance calculated
directly from the full-analysis draws was retained as a numerical check on the
additive decomposition; small discrepancies represent Monte Carlo error rather
than an additional source of uncertainty.
